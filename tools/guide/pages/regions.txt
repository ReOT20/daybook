# Regions
> Fill each region, equal digits never touch

## How to play

Tap a square, then tap a digit. Tap the digit again to clear it.

On a keyboard, the arrows move, 1 to 5 place a digit, and Backspace clears.

A repeat, or a touching pair, is shown in red as you go, but nothing stops you placing it.

Stuck? The first tap on Hint says where to look, and the second explains why. You make the move yourself, unless you ask for "Show me". If a digit you placed is wrong, Hint points at that first. Every board has exactly one answer, and you never need to guess to find it.

## Rules

- A region of N squares holds the digits 1 to N, each once.
- Equal digits may not touch, not even at a corner.
- The digits you start with are given, and stay put.

## What changes per tier

- Standard: 5x5 grid. The first three techniques below are all it needs.
- Hard: 7x7 grid. Needs a digit locked beside one square at least twice.
- Expert: 9x9 grid. Needs a digit locked beside one square at least six times.

## Techniques

These are the steps the hints teach, simplest first. Try them in this order. On each board, the colours and thick walls are the regions, the darker tint is where to look, rings mark the digits the reasoning leans on, and the filled square is the move. Every board here is a real position from the game, and this is the step the hint gives on it.

### last-square | The last square in a region

A region with one empty square is missing one digit, so that digit goes there.

~~~board
. . . . 3
1 . 1 2 .
. 2 3 4 .
. . 1 5 2
. . . 3 .
regions: aabcc adbbc dddec dffec ffeee
focus: r1c3-r2c3 r2c4
answer: r1c3=3
caption: This region has three squares, so it holds 1, 2 and 3. It has the 1 and the 2, so its empty square is 3.
~~~

### only-digit | A square with one digit left

Start from a square. It can only hold digits up to its region's size, and not one its region already has or one touching it, corners included.

~~~board
. . . . 3
1 . . . .
. 2 3 4 .
. . 1 5 2
. . . 3 .
regions: aabcc adbbc dddec dffec ffeee
focus: r2c3
cite: r3c2 r3c3
answer: r2c3=1
caption: This square's region has three squares, so it holds 1, 2 or 3. The ringed 2 and 3 touch it, so it's 1.
~~~

### only-place | A digit's only place in a region

Start from a digit a region still needs. Every other empty square in the region may have that digit touching it, and then there is one place left.

~~~board
4 . . . 5
3 . . . .
. 1 . . 1
. . . . .
. 2 . . 5
regions: aaabb cabbb cadde ccdee cddee
focus: r1c1-r1c3 r2c2-r3c2
cite: r2c1
answer: r1c3=3
caption: This region of five still needs a 3. Its two other empty squares both touch the ringed 3, so the 3 goes here.
~~~

### locked-candidate | A digit locked beside one square

Take a digit a region still needs, with a few places left. If every one of those places touches the same square outside the region, that square can't hold the digit, whichever place it ends up in. That can leave the square one digit, or leave its own region's digit one place.

~~~board
1 . . . . . 2
. . . . . 3 .
2 . . . 2 . .
. . . . . . 3
5 . . . 3 . 1
. . . . . . .
3 . 3 . 3 1 3
regions: abccddd abbccdd aabeeef gahheff gghheff gihjjkk giijjkk
focus: r4c1-r7c1 r5c2-r6c2
cite: r3c1 r5c2 r6c1
answer: r6c2=1
caption: The region down the left edge needs a 2, and the ringed 2 above it rules out its top square, so it goes in one of the other two ringed squares. Both touch this square, so it isn't a 2. Its region of three has a 3, so it's 1.
~~~
