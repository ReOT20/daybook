# Dominoes
> Lay every domino so each region keeps its rule

## How to play

Drag a domino from the tray onto a square and on into its neighbour: the way you swipe is the way it lies.

Or tap it (again to turn it round), then tap two neighbouring squares. Its first half goes on the first square.

Tap a laid domino to lift it, and double-tap or long-press it to turn it round.

On a keyboard, the arrows move, ] or Tab and [ pick a domino, F or Space turns it, Enter marks a square and lays it, and Backspace lifts.

A region's badge turns green when the region is covered and keeps its rule, and red, after a moment, once it can't.

Stuck? The first tap on Hint says where to look, and the second explains why. You make the move yourself, unless you ask for "Show me". Every hint ends in a placement. Every board has exactly one answer, and you never need to guess to find it.

## Rules

- Lay every domino from the tray on two neighbouring squares, either way round.
- Each coloured region has a rule. A number is the total of its pips; < and > bound the total.
- = means every square of the region shows the same; ≠ means no two match.
- Squares outside a region have no rule, and a domino may cross from one region into another.

## What changes per tier

- Standard: 5x5 board, 6 or 7 dominoes. Needs what a region's values allow.
- Hard: 6x6 board, 9 or 10 dominoes. Needs counting the halves left in the tray.
- Expert: 7x7 board, 12 to 14 dominoes. Needs a what-if.

## Techniques

These are the steps the hints teach, simplest first. Each board shows the tray under the grid, with each region's rule in the corner of its top-right square. The tinted squares are where to look, rings mark what the reasoning leans on, and the filled squares and tray domino are the move. A line joins the halves of a domino already laid. Every board here is a real position from the game, and this is the step the hint gives on it.

### only-partner | A square with one partner left

A square with one free neighbour shares a domino with it. Then see which domino the rules let lie there.

~~~board
. _ _ _ _
. _ _ . _
. . _ . _
. . . . .
_ _ . _ _
_ _ _ _ _
3-0 5-0 1-1 2-1 3-2
6-4 _ _ _ _
regions: a.... b..c. bd.c. b.e.f ..... ..... ..... .....
focus: r2c4 r3c4
corners: r1c1=3 r2c1=4 r2c4== r3c2=>5 r4c3=>1 r4c5=>2
answer: r2c4=1 r3c4=1 r7c3=1-1
caption: The top tinted square has one free neighbour, so the two share a domino. They're both in the equal region, so it has to be a double, and the tray's only double is 1-1.
~~~

### only-place | A domino with one place left

Take one domino and try it everywhere. If every other place breaks a rule, or strands a square with no partner, it goes in the one place left.

~~~board
_ 3 . . _
_ 5 . . _
_ . _ . .
_ . _ 2 _
. . _ 2 _
_ _ _ _ _
0-0 1-0 5-0 2-1 6-1
_ _ _ _ _
regions: .ab.. .ccd. .c.ef ...e. g..h. ..... ..... .....
focus: r1c4 r2c4
corners: r1c2=>2 r1c3=<1 r2c3=16 r2c4=<2 r3c4=<3 r3c5=>0 r5c1=<1 r5c4=2
lines: r4c4-r5c4 r1c2-r2c2
answer: r2c4=1 r1c4=2 r7c4=2-1
caption: Lay the 2-1 anywhere else and a rule or a square's only partner breaks. So it lies here, and the lower square is under 2, so that's the 1.
~~~

### only-domino | The one domino that fits a square

Take one square and go through the tray. If every domino but one is ruled out, that domino covers it.

~~~board
_ _ . . .
6 . . 4 .
6 . . 5 _
_ _ _ _ _
_ _ _ _ _
_ _ _ _ _
0-0 2-0 2-2 5-2 _
_ _ _ _ _
regions: ..... ..aab ccdd. ..... ..... ..... ..... .....
focus: r2c2 r2c3
corners: r2c4=>8 r2c5=<1 r3c2=6 r3c4=5
lines: r2c4-r3c4 r2c1-r3c1
answer: r2c2=2 r2c3=5 r7c4=5-2
caption: The region over 8 already has a 4, so the right tinted square must beat 4, and the only 5 or 6 left is on the 5-2. Its 2 can't go below, where the total of 5 needs a 0, or above, which would strand the top row's middle square. So it goes on the left.
~~~

### region-value | What a region's values allow

Work out what a region can be made of, from what the tray still has. That can rule out dominoes well away from where you started.

~~~board
_ _ . . .
. . . . .
. . . . _
_ _ _ _ _
_ _ _ _ _
_ _ _ _ _
0-0 2-0 2-2 5-2 5-4
6-6 _ _ _ _
regions: ..... ..aab ccdd. ..... ..... ..... ..... .....
focus: r2c4 r3c4
corners: r2c4=>8 r2c5=<1 r3c2=6 r3c4=5
answer: r2c4=4 r3c4=5 r7c5=5-4
caption: The tray's values can make a total of 5 only as 5 and 0. That leaves one way to cover the top tinted square: the 5-4, with its 5 on the bottom one.
~~~

### counting | Counting the halves left in the tray

Count how many halves of a value are left, and how many squares can take nothing else. If those match, no other square gets that value.

~~~board
_ _ _ _ _ _
. . . . _ _
. . _ . . _
. . _ _ . _
. . . . . _
_ . . _ _ _
_ _ _ _ _ _
2-0 5-0 3-2 4-2 5-2 4-4
5-4 6-4 6-5 _ _ _
regions: ...... .abc.. de..f. gh..i. ..j.k. .lm... ...... ...... ......
focus: r4c5 r5c5
corners: r2c2=>4 r2c3=>4 r2c4=>1 r3c1=>3 r3c2=>5 r3c5=>2 r4c1=2 r4c2=>4 r4c5=>3 r5c3=>5 r5c5=>4 r6c2=<3 r6c3=>3
answer: r4c5=4 r5c5=5 r9c1=5-4
caption: The two squares over 5 can only be 6s, and the tray has just two 6s, so no other square gets one. That leaves one way to cover the top tinted square: the 5-4, with its 5 on the bottom one.
~~~

### what-if | A what-if

Take a square with two or three dominoes that could cover it, and suppose each in turn. If all but one run into trouble within a few steps, it's the one left.

~~~board
_ _ _ _ _ _ _
_ _ _ _ _ _ _
_ _ _ . . . .
_ _ _ . . . .
_ . . . . . .
_ _ . . . . .
_ _ . . . . .
_ _ _ _ _ _ _
0-0 1-0 4-0 6-0 1-1 2-1 4-1
6-2 5-3 6-4 5-5 6-5 _ _
regions: ....... ....... ...abcd ...ef.g ...hi.j ..klm.n ..llll. ....... ....... .......
focus: r3c5
corners: r3c4=<2 r3c5=>4 r3c6=<1 r3c7=>3 r4c4=>3 r4c5=>5 r4c7=>5 r5c4=>5 r5c5=>4 r5c7=<1 r6c3=2 r6c4=22 r6c5=<1 r6c7=<1
answer: r3c5=5 r4c5=6 r10c5=6-5
caption: Every other domino that could cover this square runs into trouble within a few steps. So the 6-5 goes here, its 6 on the square below.
~~~
