# Pearls
> One loop through every pearl

## How to play

Drag through squares to draw the line. Drag back along a line to rub it out. Tap between two squares to cross that edge out: a cross is only a note, for an edge you know the loop doesn't use.

On a keyboard, the arrows move, Shift and an arrow draws or rubs out the line that way, and X then an arrow crosses that edge.

After a moment, red marks what can't stand: three lines at a square, a pearl its lines already break, or a closed loop that leaves something out. Nothing stops you drawing it.

Stuck? The first tap on Hint says where to look, and the second explains why. You make the move yourself, unless you ask for "Show me". Every board has exactly one loop, and you never need to guess to find it.

## Rules

- Draw one closed loop through the middles of squares, moving up, down, left or right. It never crosses or branches.
- The loop passes through every pearl. It need not visit every square.
- On a black pearl the loop turns, and goes straight on through the next square on both sides.
- On a white pearl the loop goes straight through, and turns on at least one of the squares just before or after it.

## What changes per tier

- Standard: 6x6 grid. The pearls' and squares' own rules finish it.
- Hard: 8x8 grid. Needs the rule that there's only one loop.
- Expert: 10x10 grid. Needs a what-if.

## Techniques

These are the steps the hints teach, simplest first. On each board, the tinted squares are where to look, grey lines and crosses are already drawn, and the coloured line or cross is the move. Every board here is a real position from the game, and this is the step the hint gives on it, except where one says otherwise.

### black-pearl | A black pearl's straight arms

A black pearl turns, and runs straight on through the next square each way. An arm that can't do that (the edge of the board, a cross, another black pearl next door) sends it the other way, and a line out of a black pearl always carries on.

~~~board
b . . . . .
. . . w . .
. . w w . .
w . . . w .
. . b . . w
. . . . . .
draw: pearls
focus: r1c1
moves: r1c1-r1c2 r1c2-r1c3 r1c1-r2c1 r2c1-r3c1
caption: In the corner, this black pearl can't go up or left. So it runs right and down, two squares each way.
~~~

### white-pearl | A white pearl's straight line

A white pearl goes straight through. If it can't go one way, it goes the other, and the sides are crossed.

### pearl-ways | A pearl with two ways in

The loop passes every pearl, and needs two ways in to do it. A pearl with only two ways left takes both.

The hints don't name this one: whenever it applies, the pearl's own rule above says the same thing first. It's still a quick thing to spot.

~~~board
b . . . . .
. . . w . .
. . w w . .
w . . . w .
. . b . . w
. . . . . .
draw: pearls
focus: r1c1
moves: r1c1-r1c2 r1c1-r2c1
caption: This corner pearl has only two ways in, so both are lines. A hint on this board names the black pearl's rule instead, and takes the lines further.
~~~

### what-if | A what-if

Suppose a line on an edge, or a cross, and follow the rules a few rounds. If something breaks, your supposition was wrong, so it's the other one. This isn't guessing: you only mark the edge once the other choice has been shown to break.
