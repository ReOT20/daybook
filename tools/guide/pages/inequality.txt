# Inequality
> Each digit once per line, signs point to the smaller

## How to play

Tap a square, then tap a digit. Tap the digit again to clear it.

On a keyboard, the arrows move, 1 to 6 place a digit, and Backspace clears.

A repeat, or a sign that is false, is shown in red as you go, but nothing stops you placing it.

Stuck? The first tap on Hint says where to look, and the second explains why. You make the move yourself, unless you ask for "Show me". If a digit you placed is wrong, Hint points at that first. Every board has exactly one answer, and you never need to guess to find it.

## Rules

- Fill every square with a digit from 1 to the size of the grid.
- No digit may repeat in a row or a column.
- A sign between two squares points at the smaller one: the narrow end is the smaller digit.
- The digits you start with are given, and stay put.

## What changes per tier

- Standard: 4x4 grid, digits 1 to 4. The first five techniques below are all it needs.
- Hard: 5x5 grid, digits 1 to 5. Needs two squares sharing two digits at least once.
- Expert: 6x6 grid, digits 1 to 6. Needs two digits with only two places at least once.

## Techniques

These are the steps the hints teach, simplest first. Try them in this order. On each board, the tinted squares are where to look, rings mark the digits the reasoning leans on, and the filled square is the move. Every board here is a real position from the game, and this is the step the hint gives on it.

### last-square | The last square in a line

A row or column with one empty square is missing one digit, so that digit goes there.

~~~board
4 . 1 .
1 . . .
2 1 . .
3 4 2 .
edges: r1c3-r1c4=< r2c1-r2c2=< r3c1-r3c2=> r4c1-r4c2=< r2c3-r3c3=< r3c1-r4c1=<
focus: r4c1-r4c4
answer: r4c4=1
caption: Row 4 has 3, 4 and 2, so its last empty square is the 1.
~~~

### only-digit | A square with one digit left

Start from a square. Its row and column may between them hold every digit but one.

~~~board
4 . 1 .
1 . . .
2 1 . .
3 4 . .
edges: r1c3-r1c4=< r2c1-r2c2=< r3c1-r3c2=> r4c1-r4c2=< r2c3-r3c3=< r3c1-r4c1=<
focus: r4c3
cite: r1c3 r4c1 r4c2
answer: r4c3=2
caption: Its row holds 3 and 4, and its column a 1, so only 2 is left.
~~~

### only-place | A digit's only place in a line

Start from a digit a line still needs. Every other empty square in the line may already have that digit in its own row or column.

~~~board
4 . . .
. 4 . .
. . 4 .
. . . .
edges: r1c3-r1c4=> r2c3-r2c4=> r3c2-r3c3=< r3c3-r3c4=> r1c1-r2c1=> r2c1-r3c1=>
focus: r4c1-r4c4
cite: r1c1 r2c2 r3c3
answer: r4c4=4
caption: Row 4 needs a 4. The ringed 4s are in columns 1, 2 and 3, so it goes at the end of the row.
~~~

### sign-bound | A square the signs leave one digit

Now the signs come in. A sign cuts a square's digits: the bigger end can't be the lowest digit the smaller end could be, or lower, and the smaller end can't be the highest the bigger end could be, or higher. Follow a chain of signs and the bottom of it loses the top digits, one per link.

~~~board
4 . . .
1 . . .
2 1 . .
3 . . .
edges: r1c3-r1c4=< r2c1-r2c2=< r3c1-r3c2=> r4c1-r4c2=< r2c3-r3c3=< r3c1-r4c1=<
focus: r4c2
cite: r3c2 r4c1
answer: r4c2=4
caption: Its row has a 3 and its column a 1, which leaves 2 or 4. The sign says it is bigger than the ringed 3 on its left, so it's 4.
~~~

### sign-place | A digit's only place, signs counted

The same search for a digit's place in a line, counting the signs. A square at the bigger end of a sign can never be a 1, and one at the smaller end can never be the top digit.

~~~board
4 . . .
1 . . .
2 1 . .
3 4 . .
edges: r1c3-r1c4=< r2c1-r2c2=< r3c1-r3c2=> r4c1-r4c2=< r2c3-r3c3=< r3c1-r4c1=<
focus: r1c1-r1c4
cite: r3c2
answer: r1c3=1
caption: Row 1 needs a 1. The ringed 1 keeps it out of column 2, and the last square is bigger than its neighbour, so it can't be 1. That leaves this square.
~~~

### naked-pair | Two squares sharing two digits

Two squares in a line that can only be the same two digits take both of them, even if you can't yet say which goes where. No other square in that line can be either digit. Count the signs when working out what each square can be.

~~~board
5 . . . .
3 . . . .
4 . . . .
1 . . . .
2 . . 5 .
edges: r2c1-r2c2=< r3c4-r3c5=< r4c3-r4c4=> r5c2-r5c3=> r4c1-r5c1=< r4c2-r5c2=> r4c5-r5c5=>
focus: r2c2 r4c2-r5c2
cite: r2c2 r4c2
answer: r5c2=3
caption: The signs leave both ringed squares only 4 or 5: the upper is bigger than the 3 beside it, and the lower is bigger than a square that is itself bigger than its neighbour. They take both from column 2, so the square below, which could be 3 or 4, is 3.
~~~

### hidden-pair | Two digits with only two places

The same pair, seen from the digits. If two digits a line needs fit only the same two squares, those squares hold them both and nothing else. That can leave another digit with one place.

~~~board
. 2 1 . . .
. 5 . . . 6
. . 5 . . .
. . . . . .
. . 6 . . .
. . . . . 3
edges: r1c2-r1c3=> r1c5-r1c6=> r4c2-r4c3=> r4c4-r4c5=> r6c2-r6c3=> r6c4-r6c5=> r4c4-r5c4=< r4c5-r5c5=>
focus: r4c1-r4c2 r4c6
cite: r4c1 r4c6
answer: r4c2=6
caption: In row 4, the columns and signs leave the 1 and the 5 only the two ringed squares, so those hold nothing else. The signs and the 6 in column 3 keep a 6 out of the middle of the row, so the 6 goes here.
~~~
